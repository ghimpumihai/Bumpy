## Decision log

Decisions D1–D17 were researched and then reviewed by an independent advisor model before being accepted. D18 and D19 are scope cuts made by the team. D20 is a correctness fix found while rechecking the plan. D21–D31 move the backend from AWS to Azure (same day); D30 also fixes gaps found in a review of the AWS version (what starts the backfill, where aggregation runs, concurrent migrations). D32–D33 make the live path (phone → cloud → dashboard) the main path. D34–D36 come from the final research pass (02 §20). D38 builds the laptop fallback upload and the finalize job's Parquet output.

| D40 | No GPS privacy trim: tracks, events, segment passes and live positions are served whole, start and end included, and a device stays on the live view for up to 60 s after its trip ends. The `in_privacy_zone` column stays (always false for new passes) and is ignored by the read API and the aggregate | Accepted | Keep the 200 m trim at both ends (plan §11); a shorter trim such as 50 m | 02 §11 |
| D45 | Aggregation: the roughness fit includes only the vehicles linked to the car through segments both drove (directly or through a chain); any other vehicle type (a lone bike, `unknown`) has no identifiable effect, is left out, and its cells show their percentile; without such a link to the car, the percentile fallback stays. Sickness per segment counts only moving, valid windows (speed ≥ 1 m/s, phone not handled: the windows roughness uses), stored per pass as `sickness_s`/`sickness_aw_rms`; passes finalized before keep their whole duration. The session MSDV stays the whole trip | Accepted | Abandon the fit for everyone when one vehicle can't be identified (the previous behaviour); pick the reference vehicle from the data; weight sickness by whole pass duration | 02 §9, §10 |
| D46 | MSDV and roughness are computed from the fused `userAcceleration` projected on `gravity`, not from the raw accelerometer. CoreMotion's `gravity` always has length 1 g, so acceleration along it (vertical heave, what Wf weighs) can't be absorbed into it and stays in `userAcceleration`. A gravity estimate tilted by θ (sustained braking or cornering) puts an error of about θ·g into `userAcceleration`, almost all of it horizontal; the vertical part is g·(1 − cos θ) ≈ θ²·g/2, under 0.4 % of g for θ = 5°. The raw accelerometer (`accel_raw.csv`, batch `accelRaw`) is still recorded and kept to cross-check this after the real drive | Accepted | Raw accelerometer with our own gravity removal (a low-pass gravity estimate would itself eat the 0.1–0.5 Hz band MSDV needs) | 01 §3 M1/M2, 02 §9 step 6 |
| D47 | No passenger MISC ratings: the app no longer asks "how do you feel (0–10)" every 2 minutes, and the read API and dashboard show only the calculated dose (MSDV, % who may vomit), with no ratings and no rating–dose correlation. `sickness` labels from older app builds are still accepted and stored, but never returned | Accepted | Collect ratings and compare them with the dose (spec §3.7 H1) | 02 §9, §19 |
| D48 | No audio. No backend step ever used the sound level or the recordings (the surface classifier was never built), and recording a car or bus cabin is the project's biggest privacy cost. The backend drops it first: ingest no longer requires the batch's `audioLevel` or checks session.json `audioEnabled` (both are accepted and ignored while app builds that send them are still in use), the processor no longer counts or stores them, and the read API and dashboard no longer show them. The iOS app stops recording audio in its own PR. The `sessions.audio_enabled` column is left in place until then (dropping it while the old processor still writes it would break the rollout); `tools/upload` still skips `*.m4a`, which older session folders hold | Accepted | Keep the level meter for a later surface classifier | 01 §3.5, §6; 02 §10 |
| D49 | Side-to-side sway counts towards motion sickness: lateral acceleration (needs the learned forward axis) gets the Donohew & Griffin (2004) weighting (flat 0.0315–0.25 Hz, then −12 dB per octave; provisional Butterworth corners) and its dose adds to the ISO 2631-1 vertical one as energy, so MSDV = √(MSDV_z² + MSDV_y²) with k_y = 1. Every MSDV the system shows (live, series, session, segment a_w, MSI) is the combined one; `sessions.msdv_z` stays vertical and `msdv_y` holds the lateral part | Partly superseded by D50 (how Wy is built) | Vertical ISO only (D19); Wf on the lateral axis too; the whole horizontal plane (fore-aft included) | 02 §9 |
| D50 | The lateral weighting Wy is built to follow Donohew & Griffin's straight-line weighting within ±0.8 dB from 0.0315 to 0.8 Hz: a Butterworth high-pass at 0.0172 Hz (below the paper's range) and a second-order low-pass at the 0.25 Hz break with Q = 0.91 (fitted, minimax). The D49 Butterworth corners at 0.0315 and 0.25 Hz read 3 dB (29 %) low at both edges of the band the paper calls flat | Accepted | Butterworth corners at the band edges (D49); a least-squares fit (smaller average error, but up to 1.3 dB off) | 02 §9 |
